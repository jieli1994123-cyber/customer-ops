# Playbook: local service businesses (SMB)

## Who they are (in findable terms)

Independent local service businesses with a physical presence and a reputation to protect: clinics, studios, salons, repair shops, small law/accounting practices, gyms, cafés with multiple locations. Single-location independents and small multi-location groups; chains and franchises' corporate HQs are out.

## Where they are publicly listed

- Map/directory listings surfaced via web search (Google Maps data as quoted in search results and directory mirrors — Yelp, TripAdvisor, industry directories)
- Industry association member directories (dental, legal, fitness, hospitality…)
- Review platforms: the business's review pages — recent owner responses signal an engaged owner; unanswered complaint clusters signal operational pain
- Local press / "best of" lists in local media

## Search queries that work

- `"<service>" "<city>" independent OR family-owned reviews` — finds independents with review footprints
- `site:<industry-directory> <city> <service>` — association members
- `"<service>" "<city>" "now open" OR "second location"` — timing: new/expanding
- `"<category>" "<city>" hiring receptionist OR front desk` — front-office pain signal

## Qualifying signals (feed /score)

- Review volume (50+ reviews = real customer flow) and a rating they visibly care about
- Own website (not just a directory page) with online booking or a contact form
- Owner named publicly (About page, local press, responses to reviews signed with a name)

## Timing signals

- Just opened, or opened a second location
- Rating slipping with complaints about the exact problem the user's product solves (`pain-public`)
- Hiring front-desk/admin roles
- Seasonal demand spike for their category approaching

## Disqualifiers (drop at discovery)

- Franchise location (buying decisions sit at HQ)
- Permanently closed / no activity in reviews for 12+ months
- No web presence beyond an auto-generated directory stub (unreachable owner)

## Contact patterns

- Owner/operator is the buyer. Public contact: business phone and contact-page email are published by design — the website's contact page is the source of record
- Named owner via local press or signed review responses upgrades a generic contact point to a named one
- First touch by email to the published business address, addressed to the owner by name when known
